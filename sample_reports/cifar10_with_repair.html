How to read this

  • Gradient alarms compare each layer with its own history, not a fixed threshold: an alarm means the gradient moved more than 2.5 standard deviations and at least 10× away from that layer's normal, on two checks in a row.
  • Certified dead channels are a proof, not a guess: the incoming filter or the BatchNorm scale is exactly zero and the BatchNorm shift is ≤ 0, so the ReLU directly after it is closed for every input and gradient descent can never revive them. It assumes BatchNorm in training mode and that the optimizer's momentum for the channel has decayed.
  • Decayed to ~0 channels have weights under 1e-5 of the layer's typical size and a shift ≤ 0: effectively dead, the usual end state of a dead channel under weight decay.
  • Constant channels output the same value for every input: alive, but they carry no information.
  • Silent channels produced no positive output on any input, several checks in a row. They are likely dead but not proven so.
  • Repairs (only if switched on) re-initialise stuck channels and reset their optimizer state, so training can use them again.
